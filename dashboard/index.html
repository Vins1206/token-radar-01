How to read this ranking — five steps

  1. Rank is implied launch value, not size. Implied value = TVL × peer × growth × maturity × breadth. A smaller protocol outranks a bigger one whenever its factors say so — Polymarket (#7) over Bittensor dTAO (#14) is the live example.
  2. Every row prints its own derivation. The verdict column is the four factors in one line; the detail view shows the same factors as cards, with the full farm plan underneath.
  3. Check the multiple before trusting a row. The peer-evidence table below the board shows the peers behind every category's multiple — including the twelve categories that have no peer and use the global 0.21x.
  4. Readiness orders what to farm first. It is a triage score (scale 40, track record 25, momentum 20, breadth 15), not a return forecast; the board's range is 16–70.
  5. Every plan ends with the same exit. Leave if TVL falls 30% from your entry week; if a token launches, claim on day one and sell at least half in the first week.

How to read this board

Why this ranking, in one pass. The board's #1 is not its biggest protocol: Sentora Curator holds slightly more TVL than Steakhouse Financial ($2.76B against $2.75B) and had the better week (+2.4% against −2.2%), yet ranks #2 — both share the 0.52 peer multiple, and Steakhouse's longer record (543 days against 407, maturity 0.82 against 0.80) and wider deployment (11 chains against 4, breadth 1.09 against 1.04) outweigh the momentum gap. The same four factors decide every rank below it: Polymarket International sits #7 on $320.2M of TVL, ahead of Bittensor dTAO's $376.0M at #14, because its category prices at 0.52 against 0.29 and its 1,845 tracked days earn a full 1.00 maturity against 0.73. Size never decides a rank here; the factors do, and every row prints them.

The board at a glance. 178 rows: the snapshot's 40-protocol watchlist, plus the 138 largest remaining protocols that record no token (no CoinGecko id and no symbol in the snapshot), excluding wrapped, staked, bridged and exchange versions of tokens that already exist. The median row holds $27.2M of TVL and a $6.4M implied value. The top ten hold $4.34B of the board's $6.46B total implied value (67%) — the ranking is top-heavy because TVL is.

How an implied value is made, in four steps. (1) Peer multiple: take every token-bearing protocol in the same category, divide its market cap by its TVL, cap each ratio at the 90th percentile of all token-bearing protocols (2.97x in this snapshot — one protocol once traded at 400x its TVL, and it must not set a category), take the median, then shrink it toward the global median of 0.21x with weight n/(n+8), and clamp the result to 0.03–2.50. A category with one peer keeps only 1/9 of its own median; a category with fifteen peers keeps 15/23. (2) Growth: add 40% of the clamped seven-day TVL change (−50% to +100%), so a fast grower can outrank a stagnant giant. (3) Maturity: 0.72 to 1.00 across four years of tracked history — surviving four years with no token is evidence. (4) Breadth: 1.5% per extra chain, up to six. Multiply the four: implied value = TVL × peer × growth × maturity × breadth. Readiness (0–100) is a separate triage score: scale, track record, momentum, breadth. Farm plans are written per protocol, not per category: six steps that name the protocol's own TVL, seven-day momentum, tracked age, chains and readiness, with an eight-week timeline and the same 30% exit trigger.

Readiness, worked for #1. Steakhouse Financial scores 70 of 100: 39 of 40 for scale (log TVL), 9 of 25 for track record (543 of 1,460 days), 6 of 20 for momentum (−2.2% in seven days), 15 of 15 for breadth (six or more extra chains). The board's readiness range is 16–70 — no protocol maxes it, because no pre-token protocol in this snapshot is simultaneously huge, old, hot and everywhere.

Worked example — rank #1, Steakhouse Financial. TVL $2.75B × peer 0.521 (Risk Curators: one token-bearing peer, its ratio capped at 2.97x, then shrunk 8/9 of the way toward the global 0.214x) × growth 0.991 (TVL −2.2% in seven days) × maturity 0.824 × breadth 1.090 (11 chains; breadth counts at most six extra chains) = $1.28B implied. Every factor is printed on the protocol's own board row and detail view, so the arithmetic can be checked without trusting this paragraph. Factors are printed to three decimals and the board's arithmetic uses the unrounded values, so recomputing from the printed factors lands within half a percent of the stated implied value.

What would have to change. Three stress tests, computed from the same snapshot. (1) If Risk Curators' single token-bearing peer vanished, the category multiple would fall to the global 0.21x and Steakhouse's implied value would drop from $1.28B to $524.3M — the top of the board survives, halved. (2) If peer ratios were not winsorized, the Prediction Market peer Rain (market cap 400.2x its TVL) would push its category to the 2.50 clamp and Polymarket's implied value from $166.0M to $797.0M, rank #3 — the cap exists precisely so one outlier cannot do that. (3) If the growth weight were 0 instead of 0.40, the top ten's membership would not change at all: growth reorders rivals inside the top ten, it does not admit new ones. The ranking is most sensitive to the peer evidence, less to maturity and breadth, and least to the growth weight.

What this board does not claim. Risk Curators' 0.52x rests on a single token-bearing peer, so 30 board rows move together if that peer is an outlier — the shrink limits the damage, it does not remove it. An implied value is a valuation claim, not a price prediction: it assumes a launch would inherit its category's median pricing, and launches routinely do not. “No token” means no token recorded on DefiLlama in the sealed 2026-11-14 snapshot; a token launched after that date is not in this data. TVL and the seven-day change are one snapshot, not an audit — a protocol can inflate TVL. Readiness is a triage score, not a return forecast.

The exit rule is part of every plan: leave any farm whose TVL falls 30% from your entry week, and if a token launches, claim on day one and sell at least half into the first week. A radar that never tells you to leave is a trap, not a tool.

Risks & limits — an analyst's note

What could make this board wrong, in order of how much it matters. Each risk carries the number that sizes it.

  1. Single-peer concentration. Twelve categories rest on exactly one token-bearing peer, and 30 board rows — the whole Risk Curators category, including ranks #1 to #3 — share that one peer's multiple. If the peer is an outlier, the category moves together. The shrink limits the damage (a 400x peer becomes 0.52x, not 2.50x), and the stress test sizes it: removing the peer halves rank #1 from $1.28B to $524.3M.
  2. TVL is unaudited. The snapshot's TVL is what protocols report through DefiLlama; a protocol can inflate TVL, and the board would rank it higher for it. No on-chain verification is part of this entry, and the seven-day change is a single window — a one-week wash of deposits reads as growth.
  3. The snapshot is one day: 2026-11-14. A token launched after that date, a TVL collapse, or a fee switch since then is not in this data. “No token” is as-of, not forever — re-check any row before farming it, and the board's own exit rule (leave at −30% TVL) is the protection against a snapshot that has gone stale.
  4. Launch pricing is an assumption, not a law. Implied value assumes a launch inherits its category's median pricing; launches routinely do not. The Polymarket stress test shows the spread: without the winsor cap its implied value would be $797.0M instead of $166.0M. Treat every implied value as a claim to argue with, not a price to expect.
  5. Costs and gas are approximations. Farm-plan costs use per-chain fee bands (Base roughly $0.05–$1.50 across a plan, Ethereum mainnet roughly $8–$35), not live quotes; a congested week costs more, and a plan that costs more than its expected points is a bad plan no matter its rank.

This board is a radar, not advice: it finds candidates and prices them one way. It does not tell anyone what to buy, and it never promises a token, a drop, or a return.
